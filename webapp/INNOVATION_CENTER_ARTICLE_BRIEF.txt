Project identity
- Base project: GPT Researcher
- Current customized project: GPT Researcher adapted for Innovation Center strategic intelligence workflows
- Main stack:
  - Python + FastAPI backend
  - HTML/CSS/JavaScript frontend pages
  - GPT Researcher as research engine
  - Azure OpenAI support
  - Web research plus local document ingestion

High-level summary
- This project started from GPT Researcher, an open deep research agent designed for web and local-document research.
- On top of that base, the project was adapted to serve an Innovation Center use case.
- Instead of only generating generic research reports, it now supports two specialized interfaces:
  - IC Trend Scout
  - IC Technology Radar
- These interfaces turn scattered signals from the web and local PDFs into structured recommendations that are easier for an innovation team to review, compare, store, and act on.

- The article should present this project not just as an AI research tool, but as an operating layer for innovation intelligence.
- The strongest framing is:
  - The solution helps an Innovation Center move from ad hoc scanning to structured, repeatable, source-linked decision support.
- It is useful because it does not stop at "research summary."
- It transforms signals into:
  - trends

  - repeatable monitoring cycles
  - historical memory across runs

What problem this solves for an Innovation Center
- Innovation teams often face these issues:
  - too many weak signals spread across the web
  - difficulty connecting news, research, startups, tools, and internal interests
  - lack of repeatable criteria for deciding what to monitor, test, or adopt
  - poor traceability between a strategic recommendation and the source evidence behind it
  - difficulty preserving historical context across recurring scans
- This project addresses those issues by:
  - collecting web and local-document evidence
  - structuring findings into reusable research flows
  - classifying outputs by strategic relevance and maturity
  - linking recommendations back to sources
  - persisting runs for later review and comparison

Why this helps a sector focused on innovation
- An Innovation Center needs more than information retrieval.
- It needs a way to identify where to invest attention, experimentation, PoCs, assets, and future offerings.
- This project supports that by turning research into portfolio-oriented outputs.
- It is especially useful for:
  - PoC funnel prioritization
  - emerging technology monitoring
  - offer discovery
  - scalable asset ideation
  - market and client signal interpretation
  - strategic technology radar creation
- In practice, the system helps an innovation function answer:
  - What should we monitor?
  - What should we test next?
  - What is mature enough for adoption?
  - What still needs assessment?
  - Which sources actually support these conclusions?

Specialized interfaces added in this project

1. IC Trend Scout
- Route: /ic-trend-scout
- Goal:
  - Research a topic and convert it into a structured innovation intelligence output.
- What it does:
  - accepts a research topic
  - accepts Innovation Center context
  - accepts keywords
  - accepts curated web sources
  - accepts local PDFs and documents
  - generates an executive summary
  - identifies key signals
  - identifies use cases and suggested PoCs
  - highlights risks and barriers
  - generates project trends connected to evidence
  - scores those trends
  - classifies them into radar rings
- Why it matters:
  - It helps innovation teams quickly convert a topic such as AI Agents, Open Gateway, Digital Twins, Synthetic Data, Quantum, or Space Tech into action-oriented insights.

2. IC Technology Radar
- Route: /ic-technology-radar
- Goal:
  - Generate a Thoughtworks-inspired radar adapted to Innovation Center needs.
- What it does:
  - uses the same research setup foundation
  - creates radar entries from evidence and context
  - organizes entries into quadrants
  - organizes entries into rings
  - turns trends into project opportunities
  - saves radar boards and historical runs
  - accumulates trends over time
  - lets users remove trends from the board
  - provides a visual radar map with numbered trends and matching textual references
- Why it matters:
  - It transforms research into a portfolio view that helps innovation leadership decide what to adopt, trial, assess, or treat with caution.

Scoring model used in the specialized flows
- Both specialized flows use four practical scoring dimensions:
  - Strategic Impact
  - Market & Client Potential
  - Execution Readiness
  - Scalable Asset Potential
- These dimensions make the output more useful for innovation governance because they balance:
  - strategic value
  - commercial opportunity
  - feasibility
  - reuse potential

Radar ring logic
- Rings used in this project:
  - Adopt
  - Trial
  - Assess
  - Caution
- Recommended interpretation in the article:
  - Adopt: mature and strategically relevant enough to use with confidence
  - Trial: promising and ready for experiments or PoCs
  - Assess: relevant but still requires study or validation
  - Caution: high uncertainty, weak fit, or meaningful risk

Radar quadrant logic
- Quadrants used in this project:
  - Techniques
  - Tools
  - Platforms
  - Languages & Frameworks
- The article can explain that the quadrants help cluster different kinds of trends so decision-makers do not look at all signals in the same way.

Persistence and historical memory
- One of the most important innovation-specific adaptations is persistence.
- The project does not only generate one-off outputs.
- It stores:
  - trend research runs
  - recurring monitor definitions
  - radar setups
  - radar boards
  - historical radar runs
- This matters for innovation because a trend only makes sense over time.
- Historical memory enables:
  - trend evolution tracking
  - comparison between rounds
  - traceability of recommendations
  - a growing institutional memory

Recurring monitoring
- The IC Trend Scout includes recurring monitors.
- A monitor can rerun a saved configuration on an interval basis.
- The current implementation supports scheduled reruns in day intervals, with a default use case around every 3 days.
- Why this matters:
  - innovation scanning should not depend on manual repetition
  - recurring runs help maintain a continuous watch on emerging themes
  - every run is stored, creating a time-based research record

Why the combination of web and local PDFs is strategically important
- Innovation decisions often require combining:
  - external market signals
  - research papers
  - internal material
  - curated strategic sources
- This project supports that hybrid model.
- It means the Innovation Center can mix public signals with internal reports or local knowledge documents.
- That increases relevance and reduces the gap between broad market hype and the organization's own priorities.

How the project uses AI in a practical way
- The system uses LLM-based research orchestration, not only direct prompting.
- The base GPT Researcher architecture works with planner and execution logic:
  - planning research questions
  - gathering sources
  - summarizing findings
  - writing structured outputs
- In this customized version, the AI is guided with domain-specific prompts for Innovation Center outcomes.
- This is important for the article:
  - The differentiation is not only "AI searches the web."
  - The differentiation is that AI is shaped into a repeatable innovation research workflow.

Role of embeddings in the project
- Embeddings are relevant because GPT Researcher uses retrieval and context compression.
- In simple terms:
  - embeddings help compare semantic similarity across chunks of text
  - they help select the most relevant context from collected documents and sources
  - they improve grounding for the final outputs
- Why this matters to the Innovation Center:
  - better retrieval means better signal quality
  - it reduces noise in large source sets
  - it helps the system work with both web content and local documents
